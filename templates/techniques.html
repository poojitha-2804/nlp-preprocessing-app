Tokenization

Tokenization breaks raw continuous text into smaller structural units called tokens (words, subwords, characters, or n-grams).

Supported Types:
Word Sentence Character Subword Whitespace Punctuation Regex N-gram BPE WordPiece Unigram
Input: "Natural Language Processing is amazing."
Output: ["Natural", "Language", "Processing", "is", "amazing", "."]

Stemming

Strips word suffixes heuristics-based to produce crude base roots (stems). Fast, but can cause over-stemming or under-stemming.

Supported Algorithms:
Porter Stemmer Snowball Stemmer Lancaster Stemmer Rule-Based Lovins Stemmer
Input: "playing played plays player"
Output: ["play", "play", "play", "player"]

Lemmatization

Uses vocabulary dictionaries and morphological context to resolve canonical base headwords (lemmas).

Supported Approaches:
WordNet Standard POS-Based Lemmatization Context-Aware Syntax Dictionary-Based Rule-Based
Input: "the 3 children were running fast"
Output: ["the", "3", "child", "be", "run", "fast"]

Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging

Assigns grammatical categories (Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Preposition) to words using contextual sequence taggers.

Supported Tagsets & Engines:
Penn Treebank (36 Tags) Universal POS (17 Categories) NLTK Perceptron SpaCy Contextual Lexicon Tagger
Input: "Natural Language Processing is amazing."
Output: "Natural/JJ language/NN processing/NN is/VBZ amazing/JJ ./."

Stop Word Removal

Filters out uninformative high-frequency structural words (like "the", "is", "at") to keep content-rich keywords.

Supported Approaches:
Standard English Custom Words Frequency-Based Domain-Specific Multi-Language
Input: "This is a simple example of natural language processing."
Output: ["simple", "example", "natural", "language", "processing"]
