Stemming Algorithms Breakdown

Algorithm 1
Porter Stemmer

Developed by Martin Porter in 1980. Applies a 5-step series of cascading rules to remove common English suffixes.

Example: "playing played" → "play play"
Algorithm 2
Snowball Stemmer (Porter2)

An optimized, improved iteration of the original Porter stemmer supporting multiple languages and better edge-case handling.

Example: "generously" → "generous"
Algorithm 3
Lancaster Stemmer (Paice/Husk)

An extremely aggressive stemmer that uses over 100 rules to strip suffixes rapidly.

Example: "ability" → "abl"
Algorithm 4
Rule-Based Stemming

Uses explicit regular expressions defined by domain engineers to strip specific suffix sets (`-ing`, `-ed`, `-ly`).

Example: "testing" → "test"
Algorithm 5
Lovins Stemmer

The earliest published stemming algorithm (1968) featuring 294 ending rules combined with double-consonant reduction.

Example: "getting" → "get"
